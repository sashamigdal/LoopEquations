Title:
Comparing the Decaying Turbulence Theory with Wind Tunnel Experiments

Authors:
Alexander Migdal

Comments:
21 pages, 10 figures. Code and data tables in the ancillary files and at https://github.com/sashamigdal/LoopEquations/tree/master/Mellin

Primary category: physics.flu-dyn
Cross-lists (suggested): nlin.CD, hep-th

Abstract (for the arXiv form; at most 1920 characters):
The Euler-ensemble solution of freely decaying incompressible turbulence predicts a universal, parameter-free second-order structure function as a Mellin-Barnes integral in the scaling variable $\rho=r/\sqrt{\tilde\nu(t+t_0)}$. We evaluate this integral for the odd Euler ensemble, the only marginally stable turbulent attractor, by steepest descent along Lefschetz thimbles with Gauss-Hermite quadrature, including the Stokes phenomenon: the residues of the Riemann-wall and dyadic-wall poles trapped by the thimble, and the thimbles that terminate on the paired zeros. The computation agrees with a direct contour integration to $5\times10^{-10}$. The same Riemann-wall and dyadic-wall poles that produce the log-periodic oscillations of the energy spectrum also produce log-periodic oscillations of the effective index $\alpha(\rho)$. They appear only at small separations, $\rho<C_{\min}$, with an amplitude below $1.5\times10^{-6}$, eight to nine orders of magnitude smaller than in the spectrum. For $\rho>C_{\max}$ the structure function is a convergent series in real powers of $1/\rho$, with no oscillations. Finite-size effects of a system of width $W$ are included exactly by subtracting the infrared part $k<\pi/W$ of the spectrum. We compare the theory with the Max Planck wind-tunnel measurements of Küchler, Bewley and Bodenschatz at $Re_\lambda=413$-$5779$. The small oscillations of the measured index at the largest separations are reproduced by the finite-width correction and disappear in the extrapolation to $Re_\lambda\to\infty$. The extrapolated large-scale tail of the index matches the odd-ensemble theory with a single scale parameter, $\chi^2/\mathrm{dof}=2.4$; at smaller separations the flow has not yet relaxed to the asymptotic state. The theory also reproduces the whole index curve of recent $4096^3$ direct numerical simulations.
